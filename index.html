How I grade

  1. Balance and match

    Correct exposure and white balance, then match shots so a scene feels like one continuous moment.

  2. Protect skin and key colors

    Make sure people look natural and important colors in the frame stay believable.

  3. Build the look

    Shape contrast, color and tone to support the mood of the story, not to show off.

  4. Finish

    Add subtle final touches and check the result before delivering in the format you need.

Services

Everything happens in DaVinci Resolve, from the first cut to the final grade, so your project stays consistent from start to finish.

Cinematic editing

I shape your footage into a story with a clear structure, deliberate pacing and cuts that feel natural.

Good for short films, trailers, travel films, event films and brand stories.

Color grading

I balance and match your shots, then build a look that fits the mood of the film.

Good for projects that already have an edit and need a consistent, polished look.

Motion graphics

Titles, lower thirds and transitions designed to match the style of your video.

Good for intros, interviews, explainers and branded content.

Short-form content

Vertical and social edits that keep a cinematic feel in a shorter runtime.

Good for reels, teasers, cutdowns and social campaigns.

How we work together

  1. Brief

    You share the goal, footage, references and deadline. I ask any questions I need.

  2. First cut

    I edit the story, pacing and structure, and send it for your review.

  3. Revisions

    We refine the cut together. Then I add the color grade and motion graphics.

  4. Delivery

    You receive the final video in the formats and sizes you need.

What I need from you

  • Your footage, with the camera format if you know it
  • A short description of the story or goal
  • References: videos or looks you like
  • Your deadline and where the video will be published

Pricing

The price depends on runtime, amount of footage, how much graphics work is involved and how soon you need it.

How I approach an edit

Story first

Every cut should serve the story. If a shot doesn't move it forward, it goes.

Rhythm matters

Pacing decides how a scene feels. I cut to the emotion of a moment, not just the action.

Grade for mood

Color supports the story. I match shots first, then shape a look that fits the film.

Graphics with restraint

Motion graphics should help the viewer, not compete with the footage.
